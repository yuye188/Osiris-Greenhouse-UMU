What actually went wrong

Three findings that matter more than the two skipped commands.

01 Six sensors froze — and one of them was the guard

temp_alta, temp_baja, hum_alta, hum_baja, par and rad stop changing on 22 August between 09:04 and 09:10 and never move again. Six channels failing inside six minutes is one acquisition failure, not six sensor faults.

The frozen values are plausible — 27.9 °C, 80 % RH, 542 µmol PAR — so no range check catches them. PAR frozen at 542 means permanent daylight for the rest of the record.

The consequence is worse than lost data. Every rule in the controller export aborts on Temperatura Interior Baja > 50 °C — the channel that froze at 27.5 °C. From 22 August on, that abort could not fire: 9 blocks passed 50 °C unguarded, the hottest reaching 56.4 °C.

Day 20 of 31 — so most of Phase D has centre sensors only.

02 The 1-hour washout is too short

Protocol §3 leaves the 2 h + 1 h block provisional until τ is measured in Phase A. Fitting the step response with a background trend — without one, the fit just tracks sunrise — gives τ = 2–39 min across six usable temperature trials.

Against the slowest, τ = 39 min: the 2 h treatment is 3.1 τ and reaches steady state, but the 1 h washout is only 1.5 τ where §4 asks for 3–5 τ. About 22 % of each treatment is still present when the next block starts.

Six usable temperature fits, labelled by trial. Median 16 min; only A7 (fog) and A6 (shade) fall past the 20 min a 1 h washout can absorb.

03 The 45 °C rule fires inconsistently

The house spent 105 hours above the 45 °C limit during the campaign, across 80 episodes, peaking at 54.7 °C. In 34 of those episodes (37 h) the roof vents never reached 100 % — clean evidence the rule did not act. In nine, the lights were still on.

The rule is armed: on day 1 the vents cycled with nothing programmed, and Phase C run 30 was overridden exactly as §8 prescribes. Delays ranged from 0 to 223 min.

Phases A and B ran hottest. The spikes after day 31 are the idle house with nothing programmed. Openings during a hot spell can't be credited to the rule — the schedule opens the roof vents routinely — so only the episodes where they stayed shut are unambiguous.

What to do with this data

Phases B, C and D deliver exactly the randomised actuator variation the repo's what-if and RL notebooks say they lack. Two cautions and three fixes.

  1. Fix the guard before scheduling another round. Repair the Temperatura Interior Baja probe, or re-point every rule's Propiedad at Temperatura Interior Centro with the threshold dropped to about 49 °C — centro reads ≈0.7 °C below baja when the house is hot. Until then a re-run cannot be validated, and 4–6 September already wasn't.
  2. Still outstanding after the repeat round — nine blocks: B22, B24, B53, B54 and D080 (over 50 °C again; re-run at 09:00 or 18:00), D05 and D06 (queue bug again), D95 (no actuator log), plus D062 and D086, which executed perfectly but breached 50 °C on 27 and 30 August and could never be flagged because the guard was already blind. Nothing else in Phase D's 96 blocks needs re-running — that is now verified, not assumed.
  3. Re-export the actuator series through 7 September. The logs in this folder stop at 6 Sep 20:02, so the tail of the repeat round cannot be checked.
  4. Scope to 8–31 August and prefer the centre sensors. Before 8 August not all actuators were online and the baseline is contaminated; from 22 August only temp_centro, hum_centro, co2 and dpv are real. Anything reading the other six after that date is reading a constant.
  5. Treat consecutive blocks as correlated. With a 1.5 τ washout, dropping the first 30–40 min (≈ 1 τ) of each treatment window before averaging removes most of the carry-over.
  6. Drop the 34 vents-shut overtemperature episodes. In those the house was thermally saturated, so the actuator setting is not what determined the climate.
  7. Lengthen the washout to 2 h, keep the 2 h treatment. That is the one design change this data justifies, and it answers the open question in §3 and note 1.
  8. Take LIGHTS and FOG effects from Phase B, not Phase D. In Phase D their commanded levels are 92 % and 71 % determined by time of day, so a Phase D estimate of a lamp or fog effect is an estimate of the hour. The windows, screen and recirculators are properly balanced and fine to use.
  9. Permute the block order in the next Sobol programme. Dealing consecutive Sobol points into consecutive slots is what created that confounding; shuffling the blocks after generation removes it.
